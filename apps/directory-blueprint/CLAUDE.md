# Directory Blueprint for Claude Code

This folder is an **LLM-maintained wiki plus workflow** whose one purpose is to **turn a Google Maps export into a ranked, monetizable local directory**, built the Rank Expand Academy way. It is the sibling of the Microsite Blueprint and follows the same pattern: the human sources material and approves gates, the LLM (you) maintains the wiki and builds.

Read `wiki/index.md` first on any task.

---

## 1. What you are building

A **vertical directory: one niche, one state.** Home, state hub, city pages with local FAQs, one page per listing, a claim-your-listing page, and featured slots on every city page. Static site (Astro), deployed to Cloudflare Pages, rebuilt from a CSV whenever anything changes.

Three reasons a member builds one, in this order:
1. It boosts a vertical they already own with microsites (the directory, the microsites and a GBP anchor own the same SERP three ways).
2. It rents its top spots (featured slots, $97 a year premium listings, one-time best-of placements) and can sell leads.
3. It gives them a reason to call every business in the niche ("we're connected because you have a listing on my directory"), and for operators who run directories this is their main source of SEO clients.

Display ads are out at this size. Owned microsites are listed **like any other business**, no sitewide links, no special treatment. The preference is obscured by the fact that the directory holds every listing in the state.

## 3. The pipeline (checkpointed, six gates)

Run in order. **Stop after each gate and wait for approval.** Write each gate's artifact into `output/<slug>/` and append a `log.md` line.

**Gate 1, Scope.** Run the fixed eight-question intake in [[gate-1-intake]], one question per turn, and write `01-scope.md` as the answers arrive: vertical (one Maps category), state, domain (owned, or the category shape to register; category shape is the recommendation), owned microsites (phone numbers only, [[footprint-rules]]), the publisher's name and details ([[publisher-entity]]), the look (the master template's as shipped, which is the default and the fallback, or one or two inspired websites as design pointers; [[design-seed]]; the seven in [[reference-directories]] are shown only when the member asks for other looks), and the listings source (DataForSEO pull or Outscraper export). The build folder is `output/<slug>/` with the slug `<state code, lowercase>-<category plural, kebab-case>` (`co-fence-contractors`, `fl-swimming-pool-contractors`), formed from the member's answers to questions 1 and 2 and used by every later script; the pilot's `florida-pool-builders` folder predates the rule. **Never recommend or pre-fill a niche, state, domain or name, and never question a vertical the member has chosen:** no scorecard and no alternatives unless they ask or the estimate prints a `VERTICAL FLAG` (fewer than 100 primary-category businesses in the state, or fewer than half with a website), which is told once with one question, continue or change. A member with no vertical, or who asks, gets the vertical check in [[gate-1-intake]]: `scripts/check_vertical.py` on up to three services they name, one at a time, and the first that passes is built; no rankings, no volumes. Members stall on this choice, and the job is to get them building (Shawn, 2026-10-01). With DataForSEO connected (`scripts/connect_dataforseo.py --check`), run `scripts/fetch_dataforseo.py --estimate` after question 2 (about five cents), show the cost at question 8, and pull only after a yes. With no login saved, the instruction is the Terminal helper, `python3 scripts/connect_dataforseo.py`, run by the member in their own Terminal; a connector cannot do the pull. Output `01-scope.md` and, on the DataForSEO path, `input/listings-raw.csv` plus `input/listings-raw-report.md`. Gate 2 starts once the member approves `01-scope.md`, in the same session if they say so.

**Gate 2, Clean.** Run `scripts/clean_listings.py` on `input/listings-raw.csv` (either source; the DataForSEO CSV uses the same columns). It also writes `02-domains.txt`, the kept rows' website domains, which is the file an Outscraper-path member uploads to the Emails and Contacts scraper for the Step 8 outreach ([[listings-cleaning]]), and strips Google's tracking parameters from `website`. Rules live in [[listings-cleaning]]: keep the state, drop permanently closed, drop DataForSEO rows not updated in 12 months, drop zero-review rows, keep names that contain a niche term or rows that carry the category (primary or secondary), dedupe on phone + address, flag owned and thin. Expect to keep 5 to 6% of a loose Outscraper scrape and about 20 to 25% of a targeted category export (22% on the pilot); a DataForSEO pull is filtered by category before it arrives, so most rows pass and the keep rate says nothing about the terms. A broad niche term lets the wrong trades in (`--niche "pool"` kept cleaners, supply stores and public pools), so use the words this trade puts in its business names plus `--category` (either flag alone is accepted). Gate 2 never drops a row for its trade: rows that carry the category only as a secondary Google category are kept, and the report breaks the kept rows down by `type` (primary category), counts the rows kept only by the category fallback with their top primary types, and totals the rows held by the cities that clear the floor, so the member can decide at Gate 4 (on the Colorado fence dry run of 2026-09-30, 133 of 368 kept rows were landscapers, handymen, roofers and the like). Output `02-listings-clean.csv`, `02-domains.txt` and `02-clean-report.md` (rows in, rows out, why).

**Gate 3, Enrich.** First write the **per-vertical attribute file** `output/<slug>/03-attributes.json` for the member's vertical (8 to 15 attributes, each with `key`, `label`, `faq`, `true_when` and `pattern`; the shape is in `scripts/examples/README.md`, the example there is one run's file and never a recommendation). When the Maps category is broader than the vertical, one attribute may carry `core: true` to record whether the business does the vertical's core work at all; what happens to listings that answer false is a Gate 4 decision for the member, never a default. Then, for each kept listing, fill the attributes as true / false / not enough info using `reviews_tags`, `about`, `subtypes` and the description, and write a one-voice description. When the export carries no such text, which is what the pilot found, read each business's own website instead: `fetch_sites.py`, then `build_evidence.py --patterns 03-attributes.json` (both free), then `make_batches.py --attributes 03-attributes.json` (every batch opens with the attribute table), answer each batch under the general `scripts/enrichment-instructions.md`, and let `merge_enriched.py` write the CSV only when every answer passes. The batches run as subagents, or with `scripts/run_batches.py enrich --slug <slug>`, which starts one headless Claude Code run per batch on Sonnet by default (`--model` overrides), checks each one, holds `03-cache/runner.lock` so a second runner on the same directory refuses while the first is alive, and prints one summary line at the end (the path for a session without subagents or a member at a terminal). State the projected cost **before** running, from the measured evidence size and both measured figures: about 4,000 tokens a listing on Sonnet subagents (pilot, 2026-09-29) and about 4 cents a listing reported by the headless runner on Sonnet (dry run, 2026-09-30; 16 cents a listing on the account's default model at the same pass rate, which is why the runner defaults to Sonnet). Then run a small pilot batch and read it before the rest. Batch by city, cache. After the runner finishes it writes `03-cache/review.md`, the latest reply of every batch (the runners' own notes on answers they were unsure about); read it, fix what needs fixing in the answers files, and rerun the checker before the member approves the gate. A description states the facts as presented and never names its source: no "its site says", "according to its website", "the footer lists", "on Google Maps", and no narrating of what is unknown (the attribute grid, the foot line and the disclaimer card carry the sourcing on the page). Every fact still traces to the packet (Shawn, 2026-09-30, feedback on the live pilot; `merge_enriched.py` fails a description that names its source)., never says the business "is listed on Google Maps", and opens with something specific to that business; an opener shared by four or more listings across the run fails the merge, and the batch is rerun with `--only`. Output `03-listings-enriched.csv`, `03-attributes.json` and `03-enrich-report.md`. The report states: the input rows; the attribute file and its core key; the website fetch outcome by status (the summary line `fetch_sites.py` prints); the evidence size and batch count; the runs with pass and retry counts; cost and time (the runner's summary line); the attribute tallies from the merge; the thin count before and after; and every warning left in place.

**Gate 4, Plan.** From the enriched CSV, [[site-architecture]] and the [[master-template]] (structure from the template, look from the design pointers, facts from the data; the template's own tokens ship as the default look and a pointer changes tokens, type, imagery and at most a page pattern or two, never the structure): the city list (which cities get a page, minimum listings per city), the page count, the launch-lean split (which pages enter the sitemap at launch), URL slugs, the featured-slot layout, and the **design tokens** derived from the design pointers (palette, type, card and button style, imagery, and whether an eyebrow line or icons are borrowed from a pointer in that site's treatment) per [[design-seed]], starting from the master template's tokens and recording every change. Measure the inspired site's contrast before borrowing its colors and record every correction. The reference build to weigh a pointer against is the reference site in `templates/master-reference/site/` (HTML, with screenshots beside it; its README lists the page to open for each page type). A member who took the default look at Gate 1 gets the template's tokens unchanged and the plan says so in one line, without a design discussion. When a pointer cannot be read by a script (Checkatrade and Pickleheads answer 403), ask the member for two or three screenshots of it pasted into the chat and derive the tokens from those; if none arrive, keep the master template's tokens and record that in `04-plan.md` ([[design-seed]]). The plan sets `niche.schemaType`, the schema.org type of every listed business: the closest `LocalBusiness` subtype that exists for the vertical, `LocalBusiness` when none fits ([[schema]]). It proposes the **service pages**: the attributes people search for as a service (never a trust mark such as a license, a warranty or financing unless the member asks), each with its slug, its title and its count of confirmed listings, as a decision for the member; an approved one gets a `service` block in the attribute file ([[page-types]], `scripts/examples/README.md`). The plan also sets `foldMaxKm` (default 40 km, the pilot's value; [[site-architecture]]) and states how many town pages it produces, and names the domain the site will be built with: the one the member owns, or the category-shape domain they intend to register from Gate 1, which is not pre-filling (the member chose it at Gate 1). Every decision that removes or alters a listing goes into `04-exclusions.csv` (`slug, action, reason`), never into the enriched CSV, and every such decision is the member's: the Blueprint has no default that drops a listing for what it does or does not do (dropping non-builders was a choice of one pilot in one industry, not a rule; Shawn, 2026-09-29). End the plan with a numbered table of the decisions asked of the member, each with a recommendation. Output `04-plan.md` and `04-exclusions.csv`.

**Gate 5, Build.** Copy `templates/astro/` to `output/<slug>/site/` and fill it, do not write an Astro project from scratch (`templates/astro/README.md` lists every file and what to fill). The skeleton already implements [[site-architecture]], [[page-types]], [[on-page-rules]], [[schema]] and the launch-lean sitemap filter; the fills are `src/data/site.json` (brand, domain, publisher, contact, tiers, the design tokens from `04-plan.md`, and the optional settings in `templates/astro/README.md`), `src/styles/fonts.css` (self-hosted fonts), `src/data/listings.csv` (the Gate 3 output with `04-exclusions.csv` applied by `scripts/apply_exclusions.py`, then `scripts/add_socials.py` for the `socials` column when `fetch_sites.py` found profile links; it targets this file, so it runs here and not at Gate 3), `src/data/attributes.json` (with the `service` blocks from `04-plan.md`; write each service page's `intro`, 80 to 200 words from the data and the fact sheet, and lint the built `dist/services/<slug>/index.html` with `--min-words 140 --max-words 270`, since the page's structure alone is about 60 words), `src/data/city-copy.json` (the 500 to 800 words of real local copy and the 4 to 6 FAQs per city, written by you from the data: about the town in relation to the trade, never an inventory of what the listings say, [[page-types]]), the About page copy, and any resource pages the member asked for. The site builds with the domain from `04-plan.md` (owned, or the category shape the member intends to register); `05-build-report.md` lists the registration as a launch blocker until it is done, since every canonical URL, the sitemap and the schema ids carry it. Before any copy is written, build `05-fact-sheet.md`: every statement about law, licensing, permits or weather with its source, and what the copy may not state (prices and build times, unless the member supplies a source). Facts come from official sources first: the state licensing board, city or county permit pages, the state 811 service, the state statutes, then a state agency or a university extension page. The fact-sheet writer may use web fetch and search for this (unlike the city copy runners, which are told not to). Each item records its URL and the date fetched; an item whose only source is a commercial guide or a news story is marked **unsourced** and may not appear in any copy until an official source is found (on the dry run of 2026-09-30 DORA answered 403, a Denver permit page had moved and the 811 law page was a 404, so the licensing item leaned on a commercial guide; that is an unsourced item, not a fact). City copy is written from packets (`node dump-cities.mjs [05-zip-county.json] ../05-cities.json` in the site folder, then `scripts/make_city_packets.py --niche "<niche plural>" --state <State>`, which names the directory at the top of every batch so the instructions stay general) under `scripts/city-copy-instructions.md`, as subagents or with `scripts/run_batches.py city-copy --slug <slug> --niche "<niche plural>"`, and only `scripts/check_city_copy.py --write` produces `city-copy.json`: it rejects numbers that are not in the data, sentences shared across cities and wording about how the directory was built. The ZIP-to-county file is optional: a JSON object mapping five-digit ZIP codes to county names, built from a public ZIP-to-county table, so the packets can name each listing's county; without it the script says so and uses the CSV's `county` column (blank on a DataForSEO pull), and county facts come from the fact sheet instead. A batch checked on its own is measured against every batch already written, so a runner can fix a sentence it shares with another batch; the full check names the batches to rerun. The runner writes `05-cache/review.md`, the latest reply of every batch (the runners' notes on copy they flagged, such as geography from outside the packet or an inference from a business name); read it and fix the answers files before the member approves the copy. City word floors follow the city's size ([[page-types]]); a small-town page kept by `foldMaxKm` is indexed like any city page and gets its own short band. Then the **copy gate** ([[copy-gate]]): run `scripts/lint_copy.py` on `city-copy.json` (with `--niche` and `--city-sizes`, so a town page gets its 120-word floor and 2 to 6 FAQs), on the built home, state and About pages in `dist/` (page bands from [[page-types]]: the built home page is 800 to 1,500 words because the template's structure alone is about 300; these pages draw copy from `site.json`, so lint what the visitor reads, never `index.astro`) and on every resource page; fix and rerun until every block passes. Resource pages, the buyer's guide and any local article are written with the **seo-content-engine** skill in `skills/` whenever it is available (its full eight-stage run, which needs the member's DataForSEO connection; without one, write them by hand and lint them). Shawn's ruling, 2026-09-29: use the skill when available. The skill writes a long article, so the home page carries the buyer's guide cut to its 400 to 600-word block and linted; the full article may become a resource page ([[copy-gate]]). City blocks and FAQs are never written with the skill, only linted by it. Compare the built home, state, city and listing pages with the same pages in `templates/master-reference/site/` (same sections in the same order; nothing from its content is ever copied), then run the [[design-seed]] tells checklist on them. `npm install && npm run build` must pass with zero warnings about missing data. Then audit the built pages: one H1 and a title on each, JSON-LD that parses and carries no `aggregateRating`, no broken internal link, no `noindex` page in the sitemap, no placeholder text. `featuredPreview` must be `false` in the build that ships. Tell the member how to look at it: double-click `preview-site.command` (Mac) or `preview-site.bat` (Windows) in `site/`, which serves `dist/` as it will be deployed. `site/dist/` uses root paths and shows unstyled with dead links when opened as a file, so never point the member at `dist/index.html`. `scripts/make_local_preview.py` writes a copy that opens from disk, only when the member wants to send the site to someone. Output `output/<slug>/site/` and `05-build-report.md`, which lists what was not done and what blocks the launch.

**Gate 6, Launch lean.** Apply [[launch-lean]]: strong pages in the sitemap (every city and town page, home, state, non-thin listings), thin profiles `noindex`, then deploy to Cloudflare Pages and dripfeed via Omega. Confirm the build ships `404.html` and `_headers`. Deploy with Wrangler (`npx wrangler pages deploy dist`); drag and drop stops at 1,000 files. Wrangler 4.144 and later hand Pages commands to Workers unless the project already exists, so create it once with `npx wrangler pages project create <name> --production-branch main --force`, then deploy without `--force`. The member registers the domain and logs in to Cloudflare; you never create an account or enter a credential. After the deploy run `scripts/check_live.py` and do not start the dripfeed until it passes. Output `06-launch.md` with the indexed-page plan, the ordered address list for the dripfeed, the widening tranches and the 30-day check date. If the deploy cannot happen, say so at the top of `06-launch.md` and list what the member has to do.


**Rebuild on change** (a sold slot, a claimed listing, a new city): edit the CSV or the featured flag, re-run Gate 5, redeploy. Never hand-edit generated pages.

**After launch, the mail worker** ([[mail-worker]], the course's Extra lesson "Automating Listing Requests"; optional, on the member's word, never part of a gate). A Cloudflare Email Worker gives the directory a real hello@ address: a fixed acknowledgement to every email and claim form within a minute, a Claude triage (category, matched listing, three checks, what is missing, summary, draft reply), and a review email to the member with a link to a page where they press Send or Ignore. It never changes a listing and never sends a draft on its own. Three member sayings, and what you do for each:
- **"Give me the Terminal commands to save the mail keys."** Run `python3 scripts/mail_worker.py keys --slug <slug>` and show its output as is. The member runs the printed `save-key` command in their own Terminal: it asks for the Anthropic API key after it starts (hidden), tests it and saves it to the keychain (or `~/.directory-blueprint/secrets.json` off a Mac). That order matters: a command copied after the key would replace the key on the clipboard. You never run `save-key`, never ask for a key in the chat, and never print one. Deploys use the `wrangler login` from Gate 6; a Cloudflare API token is needed only when the member deploys with a token (two Cloudflare accounts), and then `save-key cloudflare` prints the same way.
- **"Deploy the mail worker for my directory. The review emails go to <address>."** First `python3 scripts/mail_worker.py check --slug <slug>`; if the key is not saved, give the `keys` output and wait. Then `python3 scripts/mail_worker.py setup --slug <slug> --review-to <address>`: it copies `templates/worker/` to `output/<slug>/worker/`, fills `wrangler.jsonc` from `site.json` (brand, domain, hello@ on the root domain, tier prices, niche words), writes `src/listings.json`, creates the KV namespace, sets the two secrets, deploys, records `WORKER_URL`, and posts one test claim whose triage it prints. The member's own parts, before and after, are in the lesson: Workers Paid and Email Sending on the root domain before; the Email Routing rule `hello` → the worker after (it can only point at a worker that exists). After the member's test, ignore the test items with `scripts/mail_inbox.py --ignore`.
- **"Wire the claim form to the mail worker and deploy the site."** `python3 scripts/mail_worker.py wire-form --slug <slug>` sets `contact.formEndpoint` to the worker's `/claim`, then Gate 5 (build, audit) and the Gate 6 deploy with `check_live.py`. The claim page shows its sent notice on `/claim/?sent=1`. Tell the member to delete the old form at the form service. A claim that was already in that service's inbox is re-entered by posting its fields to `/claim` with `_noack=1`.
Day to day: "show me the mail queue" is `python3 scripts/mail_inbox.py --slug <slug>`; "send that one" and "ignore it" are `--send <id>` (or `--send <id> --text-file reply.txt` with wording the member asked for) and `--ignore <id>`; a reply goes out only on the member's word. The tone of the drafts is one function in `output/<slug>/worker/src/templates.ts`; after changing it, `scripts/mail_worker.py deploy` and `mail_inbox.py --retriage <id>` on the open items. After any listings change, `scripts/mail_worker.py deploy --slug <slug>` redeploys the worker with the current listings. Troubleshooting is in [[mail-worker]].

**Upkeep** ([[refresh-and-upkeep]]): no owner logins in v1; every claim is verified by hand before the CSV changes. Listing data changes on claims, sales and corrections, not on a calendar. Money-page refresh (home, state, city pages) follows the general SEO advice, which is not a directory rule.

## 4. Tech-stack rules (locked by Shawn, 2026-09-16)

- **Astro, static output, Cloudflare Pages.** No server, no database in v1. D1 optional later for claims.
- One repo per directory, `npm run build` produces `dist/`, Cloudflare Pages builds from the repo or takes the `dist/` upload.
- Every page ships complete on-page SEO and JSON-LD per [[schema]]. Real `sitemap.xml` output, always.
- No GA, no Search Console property when the directory sits beside a microsite network (footprint rule, [[footprint-rules]]).
- Claim form posts to a no-backend form service at launch, or to the mail worker's `/claim` once the member sets it up ([[mail-worker]]). Featured tiers are Stripe Payment Links pasted into `templates/featured-tiers.md`. The member sells by hand, flips the `featured`, `premium` or `best_of_rank` column, rebuilds.
- Unclaimed listings carry no photos. Photos are owner-supplied with a claim or a paid tier ([[page-types]] image policy).
- Facts are visible text: no accordions or tabs for FAQs, hours or attributes.
- Every directory is the master template ([[master-template]]): structure, page set, URLs, nav and section order come from `templates/astro/` and weigh heaviest in every build. The sites a member names at Gate 1 are design pointers for the look only (palette, type, cards, imagery, at most a page pattern or two), per [[design-seed]]. Locked by Shawn, 2026-09-30. The reference build ships as HTML in `templates/master-reference/site/` and is the most important reference and the default look when the member names no site (Shawn, 2026-10-01); its content is never reused.
- Every directory has a named publisher on About with `Person` schema, and the badge ships as an HTML embed that links to the listing page ([[publisher-entity]], [[authority-and-links]]).

## 5. Data sources

Two taught paths into `input/listings-raw.csv`, same columns ([[outscraper-columns]]), same Gate 2.

- **DataForSEO Business Listings API** (`scripts/fetch_dataforseo.py`, default when the member has an API login). Category-exact: one category in one state comes back as the reviewed businesses only, so a whole state is typically 2,000 to 8,000 rows and a few dollars (Florida swimming pool contractors: 2,028 rows, about $0.77; Texas roofing contractors: 8,203 rows, about $3, measured 2026-09-29). Rows carry attributes, review topics, the star distribution, claimed status, hours, photos and web-sourced emails and extra phones. Measured against an Outscraper pull of the same category and state on 2026-09-29: it holds 95 to 96% of the reviewed, open, primary-category businesses at one 27th of the price. Caveats to state in the FAQ: it is a database with no closure flag (Gate 2 drops rows not updated in 12 months, which removes most closed and vanished listings), about one open business in twenty is missing, a business can appear on a secondary category (Gate 2 keeps those rows and reports them by primary type; the member decides at Gate 4), and emails need a domain check and a bounce test. The login is the member's DataForSEO API login, saved once from their own Terminal: `python3 scripts/connect_dataforseo.py` (asks for the two values with the password hidden, tests them, saves them through `claude mcp add -s user dataforseo`), or the same command typed by hand from the Gear Up lesson "Connect DataForSEO to Claude" with single quotes around both values. That is the default instruction, not the connector (first live run, 2026-10-01: a connector alone gave the script nothing, the Terminal could not find `claude`, and an unquoted password came back 401; the helper removes all three). The script reads the login from `~/.claude.json` (or the environment, or a Claude Desktop stdio entry), never from a file in this folder, and never asks for it in chat; you never run the helper yourself. The script always quotes the cost before pulling. `--find-category <word>` lists the category ids, free. `--estimate` (about five cents) also reports the primary-category count and the website share and prints a `VERTICAL FLAG` only when the vertical is clearly a bad fit.
- **Outscraper export** (the video's path, no key, no code): the member scrapes the exact category in the state and drops the CSV. Fresh scrape, about $3 per 1,000 rows after 500 free, tens of thousands of raw rows, 5 to 6% kept.
- The DataForSEO **MCP tools** in the chat (from the same Terminal setup, or a claude.ai connector) are for research and the seo-content-engine skill, not for the pull: a handful of listings is 100 KB of JSON in the conversation. The pull is the script, and a connector by itself (OAuth) leaves the script without a login.
- No synthetic or demo data path. A build starts from real listings or waits for them.

## 6. Copy tools

- **`skills/seo-content-engine/`** is Rank Expand Academy's own skill (in test, 2026-09-29). Two uses here, both in Gate 5 ([[copy-gate]]): its `scripts/lint_article.py` checks (forbidden words and phrases, reading grade, word band) run on every short block through `scripts/lint_copy.py`, with no account needed; its full eight-stage workflow writes the article-shaped pages (resource pages, the home buyer's guide, local articles) and needs the member's **DataForSEO** connection. DataForSEO is optional on the member path: the launch needs none of it, and resource pages are post-launch authority work ([[authority-and-links]]). The skill detects the connection by tool name under any prefix (a claude.ai connector or `claude mcp add`), so no credential is ever written into this folder; `skills/seo-content-engine/references/credentials.md` ships blank and stays blank.
- Do not run the skill on city blocks. It writes one article per run in a long-form shape (TL;DR, verdict, 1,500 words and up); sixty city pages would be sixty runs of the wrong shape. The city block is 500 to 800 words from the data, written by you, linted by the gate.
