# ScannerFinder regulatory fact sheet

Scope: advertising and device rules that apply to listings of independent practitioners offering bioresonance, biofeedback and other device based "wellness scan" sessions (ZYTO, BICOM, Asyra/Qest4, NES Health, Rayonex, NLS/Metatron/Oberon, SCIO/Indigo/Eductor, MORA, TimeWaver, AO Scan) in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, South Africa, New Zealand and Ireland.

How to read this file:

1. Each item is one short statement, then the source name, the full URL, and the fetch date.
2. Every **sourced** item was fetched on 2026-10-12 and checked against the page text.
3. Items marked **unsourced** could not be confirmed on a live official page (blocked, timed out, 404, or only found in news, blogs or commercial guides). Unsourced items may NOT be used in copy. They are kept here only so editors know they exist and can verify them by hand.
4. None of these devices has scientific validation as a diagnostic tool. The directory writes in a wellness register, never a medical one.

## United States

**US1.** The FTC says advertisers must back health claims with "competent and reliable scientific evidence", and for health benefits it generally expects randomized, controlled human clinical testing.
Source: Federal Trade Commission, Health Products Compliance Guidance.
URL: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**US2.** Under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act section 201(h), a product is a medical device if it is intended for use in the diagnosis of disease or other conditions, or in the cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention of disease.
Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, How to Determine if Your Product is a Medical Device.
URL: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/classify-your-medical-device/how-determine-if-your-product-medical-device
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**US3.** On 21 June 2023 the FDA issued a warning letter to ZYTO Technologies (Lindon, Utah). FDA said the ZYTO Hand Cradle galvanic skin response device was cleared (510(k) K111308) only for measuring galvanic skin response, and that promoting it to identify "stressors" such as Alzheimer's disease, HIV, Parkinson's disease and melanoma, and to recommend "balancers", amounted to diagnosis and treatment claims outside its clearance. FDA's letter also lists food and environmental allergens in the software's virtual item library.
Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, Warning Letter to ZYTO Technologies, Inc., CMS 652316, 21 June 2023.
URL: https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/zyto-technologies-inc-652316-06212023
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**US4.** In November 2015 ZYTO Technologies recalled its ZYTO Select and ZYTO Elite software (and the ZYTO Tower) for "Claims Exceeding the 510(k) Clearance"; FDA classed it a Class 2 recall with the cause "Labeling False and Misleading".
Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, Medical Device Recalls database, recall entry 147046.
URL: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfRes/res.cfm?id=147046
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**US5.** Federal rules define a biofeedback device as an instrument that gives a visual or auditory signal of a person's physiological state (for example muscle activity or skin temperature) so the person can learn to control it. It is a Class II medical device; it is exempt from premarket notification only when it is a prescription battery powered device indicated for relaxation training and muscle reeducation.
Source: Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, 21 CFR 882.5050 Biofeedback device.
URL: https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-01/title-21.xml?part=882&section=882.5050
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**US6.** FDA's General Wellness guidance (issued 6 January 2026) says FDA's device center does not intend to examine low risk general wellness products to decide whether they are devices or whether they meet device requirements. Claims to "promote relaxation or manage stress" are listed as general wellness claims, provided they make no reference to diseases or conditions.
Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices (guidance PDF).
URL: https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**US7.** The same guidance applies only to products that are low risk; a product is not covered if it is invasive, implanted, or involves a technology that may pose a safety risk without regulatory controls. FDA also states that inclusion under the policy "does not establish that it has been shown to be safe and/or effective".
Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices (guidance PDF).
URL: https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**US8. unsourced.** FDA issued an earlier warning letter to ZYTO in 2015 over similar disease and supplement selection claims. Found only in trade press and ConsumerLab; the FDA letter itself was not fetched. Do not use.

## United Kingdom

**UK1.** The CAP Code (rule 12.1) requires objective health claims to be backed by evidence, where relevant including human trials. Medical claims may only be made for licensed medicines or for medical devices that carry the applicable conformity marking.
Source: Advertising Standards Authority / Committee of Advertising Practice, CAP Code Section 12.
URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/12.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**UK2.** CAP Code rule 12.2 says marketers must not discourage essential treatment for conditions that need medical supervision, and must not offer specific advice, diagnosis or treatment for such conditions unless it is done under the supervision of a suitably qualified health professional.
Source: Advertising Standards Authority / Committee of Advertising Practice, CAP Code Section 12.
URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/12.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**UK3.** The ASA's own advice on bioresonance says claims about diagnosing or treating medical conditions need robust clinical evidence, that the ASA and CAP "have yet to see any evidence" that bioresonance devices can diagnose or treat disease, and that a device's CE marking alone is not evidence of efficacy.
Source: ASA / CAP AdviceOnline, Bioresonance therapy (page dated 6 January 2026).
URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/bioresonance-therapy.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**UK4.** ASA ruling, 22 April 2026, Global Health Tests Ltd t/a Check My Body Health: complaint upheld against a bioresonance "Body Intolerance Test" using a hair sample. The ASA found no robust evidence that the test could reliably assess intolerances. Breaches of CAP Code rules 3.1, 3.7 and 12.1.
Source: Advertising Standards Authority, Ruling on Global Health Tests Ltd (A25-1317903).
URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/global-health-tests-ltd-a25-1317903-global-health-tests-ltd.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**UK5.** ASA ruling, 13 March 2019, Wayne Hardwick t/a Body Resonance: upheld. Claims that bioresonance could diagnose or relieve conditions such as eczema, Lyme disease, hayfever and insomnia were unsubstantiated, and references to conditions such as Crohn's disease and asthma could discourage essential treatment. Breaches of rules 3.1, 3.7, 12.1 and 12.2.
Source: Advertising Standards Authority, Ruling on Wayne Hardwick (A18-468020).
URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/wayne-hardwick-a18-468020.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**UK6.** ASA ruling, 10 October 2018, Bioresonance Totnes: upheld. A poster offering a bioresonance body "scan" report on organ function, "parasites" and "spinal health" made unsubstantiated medical claims; the advertiser was told not to make medical claims without a CE marked device and evidence of effectiveness.
Source: Advertising Standards Authority, Ruling on Person(s) unknown t/a Bioresonance Totnes (A18-457357).
URL: https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/person-s--unknown-a18-457357.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**UK7.** The ASA says all efficacy claims for food intolerance, sensitivity or allergy tests need robust clinical trial evidence, and that it has not seen convincing evidence that electrodermal (Vega) testing or hair analysis produces reliable results.
Source: Advertising Standards Authority, Food intolerance and allergy testing: some top tips (16 September 2021).
URL: https://asa.org.uk/news/food-intolerance-and-allergy-testing-some-top-tips-on-making-sure-the-asa-tolerates-your-ads.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**UK8.** Under the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (enforced by the MHRA), a medical device is an article intended by its manufacturer for diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease, among other medical purposes.
Source: legislation.gov.uk, The Medical Devices Regulations 2002, regulation 2.
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/618/regulation/2
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**AU1.** The Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code (section 8) requires advertising about therapeutic goods to be accurate, balanced and not misleading, to contain only information the advertiser has substantiated before publication, and (for registered goods) to be consistent with the purpose accepted on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG).
Source: Federal Register of Legislation, Therapeutic Goods (Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code) Instrument 2021, section 8.
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L01661/2022-12-20/2022-12-20/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

**AU2.** The same Code (section 9) bans advertising that presents therapeutic goods as safe without side effects, effective in all cases, a guaranteed cure, or infallible or miraculous.
Source: Federal Register of Legislation, Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code Instrument 2021, section 9.
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L01661/2022-12-20/2022-12-20/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
Fetched 2026-10-12.

## What the copy may NOT state

These limits apply in all 7 countries. Each one rests on the sourced items above.

1. **No health claims.** Do not say or imply that a session improves, supports, fixes or relieves any disease, condition, symptom or body organ. (US1, UK1, UK3, CA2, AU1, ZA1, NZ2, IE1)
2. **No diagnostic or treatment verbs.** Never write "diagnose", "detect", "test for", "screen for", "identify", "find the cause of", "treat", "heal", "cure" or "prevent" about a device or session, and never pair a device with any named disease, allergy, intolerance, infection, parasite or deficiency. Device definitions in every country turn on exactly these purposes. (US2, US3, UK8, CA4, AU3, NZ3, NZ4, ZA2, IE1)
3. **No allergy, intolerance or sensitivity "testing".** Regulators have repeatedly found these claims unsubstantiated for bioresonance and hair tests. (UK4, UK7, IE3, US3)
4. **No "scan results" framed as health information.** Do not describe reports on organ function, stressors, toxins, pathogens or "body age". (UK6, US3)
5. **No suggestion to skip or replace medical care,** and no mention of serious conditions (cancer, heart disease, asthma, Crohn's, autism, ADHD and similar). (UK2, UK5, IE2)
6. **No approval or clearance claims.** Do not say a device is "FDA approved", "FDA cleared", "TGA approved/listed", "Health Canada licensed", "CE marked as a medical device" or "registered with SAHPRA/Medsafe/HPRA" unless an official source confirms it for that exact device and that exact use. Even where a clearance exists it can be narrow: ZYTO's clearance covers only measuring galvanic skin response. A CE mark alone is not evidence that it works. (US3, US4, UK3, UK6)
7. **No endorsements** by doctors, healthcare professionals, hospitals, laboratories or government bodies. (NZ2, IE1)
8. **No "safe", "no side effects", "works for everyone" or "guaranteed"** claims. (AU2, NZ2)
9. **No prices,** fees, discounts or offers of any kind in directory copy.
10. **Do not use "biofeedback" as a softer word for bioresonance.** Biofeedback is a specific regulated device category. Only use it when the listing really offers biofeedback relaxation training. (US5)

## Safe statements for copy

Writers may reuse these sentences as written. Each is traced to sourced items.

1. "Bioresonance and biofeedback scans are offered as wellness sessions. They are not medical tests." (UK3, US2, US6)
2. "These sessions do not diagnose, treat or prevent any disease or condition." (US2, UK8, AU3, CA4, NZ3)
3. "Practitioners listed here offer relaxation and general wellness sessions only." (US6, US7)
4. "If you have a health concern, see a doctor or other qualified health professional. A wellness session is not a substitute for medical care." (UK2, IE2)
5. "Wellness scan devices have not been shown by scientific evidence to work as diagnostic tools." (UK3, UK4, UK7)
6. "ScannerFinder lists independent practitioners. We do not endorse any device, practitioner or result." (NZ2, IE1)
7. "Ask your practitioner what a session involves before you book." (CA2, NZ2)
8. "Biofeedback uses a signal from your body, such as skin temperature or muscle activity, to help you practise relaxation." (US5, US6)
